    Common questions
    
        
    FAQ
    
        
    
          
    
            Is this legal?
            
    Yes. Appealing a parking charge is a lawful right. Private operators must consider appeals, and BPA members must offer POPLA while IPC members must offer the IAS. TicketKiller UK helps you draft a structured challenge citing UK law (including POFA 2012 Schedule 4) and industry codes. We do not impersonate solicitors, lodge appeals for you, or give personalised legal advice.
    
          
    
          
    
            What if my appeal is rejected?
            
    If the operator rejects your first appeal, ask them for a POPLA code (BPA operators) or an IAS code (IPC operators). You usually have 28 days from the rejection to escalate. Do not ignore a rejection: paying the discounted rate, appealing, or escalating are the safe options. Debt collectors cannot force payment without a court claim. The results page drafts your escalation letter.
    
          
    
          
    
            How long do I have to appeal?
            
    For most private parking charges you should appeal to the operator within 28 days of the notice — but check the letter, because some quote 14 or 21 days. The reduced payment is usually offered for 14 days; appealing inside that window normally preserves the discount if you later lose. Council PCNs have their own 14/28-day windows under the Traffic Management Act 2004. The deadline tracker does this arithmetic for you.
    
          
    
          
    
            Should I name the driver?
            
    Usually no. If you are the registered keeper, you are not obliged to identify the driver. Naming them can destroy a keeper-liability defence under POFA 2012 Schedule 4. Our letters appeal as keeper and do not admit who was driving.
    
          
    
          
    
            Has the new Private Parking Code of Practice started?
            
    Not yet. The government laid a statutory Code before Parliament on 20 October 2026 under the Parking (Code of Practice) Act 2019. It is subject to a period of parliamentary scrutiny and comes into force 21 days after it is issued, with transitional arrangements published separately. Until then, operators follow their trade association's code — the BPA/IPC Single Code — and a charge is assessed under the rules in force on the date of the parking event.

            Council ticket or private charge?
            
    This tool is aimed at private Parking Charge Notices (ParkingEye, UKPC, Euro Car Parks, and similar). Council PCNs are civil penalties with a different process (informal challenge, then formal representations, then tribunal). The letter still helps you organise facts, but council appeals should follow the wording on the PCN.
